## What I work on
I work mostly on backend systems and the tooling around them. A lot of my day-to-day work is in test infrastructure, CI systems, and automation that keeps environments stable.

I’ve spent a lot of time building and maintaining tools that deal with configuration, auditing, and traceability. I also work on software and hardware integration in lab environments, where problems are often hard to reproduce and easy to misdiagnose.

## What people usually come to me for
- Owning backend or automation systems that have to keep working over time
- Untangling test or lab setups that are confusing or brittle
- Helping debug software and hardware integration issues
- Getting tools ready for reviews, audits, or formal testing
- Jumping in when things are behind or under pressure

## How I tend to work
Most of the time I’m brought in when something feels off. Tests are flaky. Builds pass but nobody trusts them. Configurations drift.

I usually start by making things easier to see. Better logs. Clearer signals. Fewer guesses. Once people can tell what’s actually happening, the system usually gets easier to fix and maintain.

## Defense and regulated work
A good chunk of my background is in defense and cleared environments. That means working with real requirements, reviews, and audits.

I’m used to documenting decisions, keeping traceability between requirements and tests, and building tools that can hold up under scrutiny without slowing everyone down.

## Infrastructure interests
Outside of work, I run a small homelab. I use Proxmox and self-host a few services.

I spend time on networking, DNS, logging, and debugging issues that only show up once multiple systems are involved. A lot of this ends up influencing how I think about testing and reliability at work.

## What I’m currently interested in
Lately I’ve been focused on making CI systems quieter and more reliable, building simple audit and change tracking tools, and making system-level tests easier to debug.
